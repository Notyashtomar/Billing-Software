How Bird billing fits together

One flight, one airline contract, two kinds of airport. This is the core path from handling the aircraft to the invoice, and from that invoice to what Bird owes the airport.

The three parties

Money moves in one direction on the invoice, and in another direction behind it.

Airline The customer. Pays Bird for the turnaround and for anything extra the contract prices separately.
Bird The ground handler. Does the work, raises the invoice, and owes the airport a concession on that work.
Airport Either a private operator (Delhi is DIAL) or AAI. Allows Bird to handle flights and takes a cut. Not the customer on Bird’s invoice.

From the flight to the invoice

Read this top to bottom. The airport type is chosen first because it decides the royalty rule. The flight itself is priced from the contract.

1. Station Delhi, Mumbai, Hyderabad, Chennai, and the others. Each station is one airport regime.
  1. Private airport — percentage royalty, plus a minimum (MAG) by aircraft size and by service.
  2. AAI airport — percentage only. Royalty on gross, or royalty on net. No per-aircraft floor.
2. Contract for that airline at that station The SGHA. Example: Virgin Atlantic at Delhi, VS-DEL-SGHA.
  1. BHS — the turnaround rate. One charge for arrival and the following departure. Cabin clean and ordinary ramp work sit inside it.
  2. ADS — extra equipment, services, and manpower. Each row has a price, a unit, a free count, and the aircraft it covers.
  3. Concession mode — Inclusive, Exclusive, or Extended. This decides whether the airport’s share is already inside the rate or added on the invoice.
  4. BME switch — bridge equipment owned by a private airport stays off Bird’s ADS invoice unless this is turned on.
3. Flight report Operations record the flight and every equipment, service, and manpower line. Spellings vary. Billing maps them to one name (GPU-180KV, Toilet Service, Wheel Chair - WCHR/S, and so on) before pricing.
4. Split the work The same flight feeds more than one invoice type. A line goes to the type that the contract prices.
BHS — basic handling One turnaround line when the flight is handled under the package. The rate depends on the aircraft. Services named in the package, including the first pushback, the first toilet, and the first 15 wheelchairs on Virgin Delhi, are not repeated as ADS lines.
ADS — additional services For each equipment line: match the contract row, count what was used, subtract the free allowance for that flight, and bill only the remainder. No match, or a remainder of zero, means no ADS line. Per-hour and per-15-minute units use the engagement time from the flight report.
5. Airline invoice BHS invoice for the turnaround. ADS invoice for the chargeable extras (on Virgin Delhi in September that was GPU and wheelchairs above the free count). Tax is added after the concession step below.
6. What Bird owes the airport Calculated from the invoice and the station regime. This amount is Bird’s cost. It appears on the airline invoice only when the contract mode is Exclusive or Extended.

Private airport and AAI, side by side

Private airport — DIAL and the others

Two numbers are compared. Bird owes the higher one.
  1. Percentage royalty on the handling amount.
  2. MAG, the floor. Look up the aircraft band (B, C, D, E, F — a larger letter is a larger aircraft) and the service that was sold.
  1. Passenger terminal only
  2. Ramp only
  3. Full handling, passenger plus ramp — the normal turnaround
  4. Cargo ramp
The AFKL sheet effective 1 April 2026 is this floor. A category E full turnaround cannot go below 38,130, even when the percentage works out lower. Bridge-mounted GPU and air-conditioning belong to the airport. They are recorded on the flight and charged by the airport, not by Bird. Bird bills its own unit when the aircraft is on a remote bay, the bridge unit has failed, or the APU is inoperative.

AAI airport

No aircraft-wise floor and no service-wise floor. Only a percentage.
  1. Royalty on gross — percentage times the invoice amount. 100,000 at 10% is 10,000.
  2. Royalty on net — the percentage is worked so Bird still keeps the agreed handling rate. The invoice gross-up is percent ÷ (100 − percent).
There is no MAG table to consult. Aircraft size still changes the airline’s handling rate. It does not set a minimum owed to AAI.

How the airport’s share shows on the airline bill

Inclusive The contract rate already contains the concession. The invoice has no extra concession line. Virgin Delhi is this case: the SGHA rates include the DIAL fee.
Exclusive Bill the handling amount, then add the royalty as its own amount. On BHS the result is also held against the ceiling for that many flights. ADS and RAF use the percentage without that ceiling.
Extended Exclusive, with royalty on net. The percentage is grossed up so the royalty is calculated on the amount Bird keeps, not only on the face of the bill.

Name matches an ADS row, used quantity is above the free count Invoice line for the excess. Wheelchairs on Virgin: 15 free per flight, then INR 850 in the SGHA (the live contract rate may differ).
Name matches, used quantity is inside the free count No ADS line. Pushback once, with 2 free, stays inside the allowance.
Ordinary ramp gear, cabin clean, or a name that is not an ADS row Stays in the BHS turnaround. Price on the ADS preparation is zero.
